# Motion philosophy

The rules behind `video.py`. The style is calm and premium, close to a product launch film.

## The feeling

Relief. Meetings move fast and notes cannot keep up. The video should feel like the moment you can stop writing and just listen.

## The story

| Part | Time | What the viewer sees |
|------|------|----------------------|
| Problem | 0 to 7 s | Meeting names roll past, a notebook fills up, the ink fades: "Notes pile up. Context slips away." |
| Promise | 7 to 8.5 s | "Meet Marginalia" |
| Proof | 8.5 to 40 s | Real screen recordings, one statement per shot: It listens. Tag what matters. The AI tidies it. It remembers. Notes, written for you. Ask anything. With sources. |
| Trust | 40 to 51 s | "All on your phone. No account. No cloud." Then the optional PC that keeps nothing |
| Close | 51 to 56 s | Wordmark and "Put the pen down." |

## Rules

- **One statement per scene.** Short, large type. The product carries the rest of the time.
- **Real app footage is the proof.** Never more than 2.4 times real speed, so captions stay readable.
- **Readable close-ups.** The phone screen grows into a large rounded card that crops to the part of the screen that matters (shared element morph), then shrinks back.
- **One continuous camera.** Slow push-ins inside a shot, eased morphs between shots. No cuts back to the start position.
- **Mask reveals for type.** Each line rises from behind an invisible edge and leaves upward the same way.
- **No bounce, no squash, no floating.** Entrances use ease-out quint (about 0.75 s), exits use ease-in cubic (about 0.4 s, shorter than entrances). Camera moves use ease-in-out cubic.
- **One hero at a time.** Lines in a block stagger by 90 ms, and nothing else moves while a statement lands.
- **Calm background.** Cream with soft warm and lavender light that does not move.
- **60 fps.**
- **Generic problem, no blame.** The opening describes what happens in every meeting, not what one person did wrong.
- **No sound.** LinkedIn plays muted. Add music in the editor if wanted.

## Wording

Plain words, no semicolons and no em dashes, the same rules as the app. The feature is called "compute node" in the app and "your own PC" in the video.
