As developers we spend a lot of time in meetings. Standups, sprint planning, design reviews, syncs.

Most of us take notes the same way: a notebook or a scratch file, written while people talk. It works in the moment. A week later the note is still there, but the context isn't. Who raised it? Why? What did we actually agree on?

Writing and listening compete for the same attention, and something always gets dropped.

I wanted AI to help with exactly that gap. Not to attend meetings for anyone, but to work like an extension of your memory, so you can stay in the conversation and still have everything later.

So I built Marginalia, an Android app that sits on the table and does the listening:

• Captions appear live while people talk
• Tap Note, Action, Decision or Question and type a few words. The AI turns them into a proper note using what was just said
• Related bits from earlier meetings show up while the meeting is still going
• Afterwards, ask "what did we decide about X?" and get the answer with the exact moment it was said

One rule from day one: it runs on the phone. No account, no cloud, no recording on someone else's server. A lot of what teams discuss at work shouldn't be uploaded anywhere.

When the phone needs more power, it can borrow your own PC on the local network. The PC does the heavy work and keeps nothing.

For the developers wondering what's inside:
Gemma 4 E2B on LiteRT-LM writes notes and answers, EmbeddingGemma powers search, and whisper.cpp (Whisper small) does live captions. On the PC: llama.cpp, Whisper large-v3 turbo, and pyannote with WeSpeaker to tell speakers apart. Core in Rust, app in Kotlin.

The goal is simple: put the pen down, look at whoever is speaking, and trust that the context will still be there next week.
