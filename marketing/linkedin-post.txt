Standups. Sprint planning. Design reviews. Syncs about the syncs.

My system was a notebook. I'd write while people talked, and by the time I finished a line, the discussion had moved on. A week later I'd find a note that made perfect sense when I wrote it and none at all now. Who said it? Why? What did we actually decide?

The notes were there. The context was gone.

I didn't want AI to attend meetings for me. I wanted it to work like an extension of my own memory, so I could listen and think, and still have everything later.

So I built Marginalia for myself. It's an Android app that sits on the table and does the listening:

• Captions appear live while people talk
• I tap Note, Action, Decision or Question and type a few words. The AI turns them into a proper note using what was just said
• Related bits from older meetings show up while I'm still in the room
• Afterwards I ask "what did we decide about X?" and get the answer with the exact moment it was said

One rule from day one: it runs on the phone. No account, no cloud, no recording on someone else's server. A lot of what we discuss at work isn't something I'm allowed to upload.

When the phone needs more power, it can borrow my own PC on the local network. The PC does the heavy work and keeps nothing.

For the developers wondering what's inside:
Gemma 4 E2B on LiteRT-LM writes notes and answers, EmbeddingGemma powers search, and whisper.cpp (Whisper small) does live captions. On the PC: llama.cpp, Whisper large-v3 turbo, and pyannote with WeSpeaker to tell speakers apart. Core in Rust, app in Kotlin.

The best part isn't any single feature. It's that I put the pen down, look at whoever is speaking, and trust that the context will still be there next week.
